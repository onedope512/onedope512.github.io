Software project · Summer 2026

StarForge

A procedural 3D space engine — full star systems from a single number

StarForge turns one seed into an entire star system: a star, its planets, and every scattered rock and dune on them. Feed it the same seed twice and you get the exact same universe back — same star class, same worlds, same terrain, down to the last placed instance. That determinism is what makes a one-person space engine possible: nothing has to be hand-placed or saved to disk, it just has to be re-derivable.

The hard part wasn't generating a planet, it was standing on one. At a scale of 47,000 units, 32-bit floating point stops being precise enough — jitter and z-fighting creep in the farther you get from the origin. StarForge fixes this by silently recentering the origin on the player every 3,000 units, so the numbers in play never get large enough to lose precision. Terrain itself streams in on multiple threads, keeping around 100,000 scatter instances (grass, rock, debris) resident at any given altitude, from orbit down to the surface.

Software project · Summer 2026

N-Body Orbital Mechanics Simulator

Real Newtonian gravity, running live in a browser tab

Every body in this simulator pulls on every other body, every physics step — real Newtonian N-body gravity, not a scripted animation. The integrator is a symplectic leapfrog (half-kick, drift, recompute, half-kick), which is the reason orbits here don't slowly spiral in or fly apart over time the way a naive Euler integrator's would. Energy is conserved indefinitely, so a stable binary system placed once will still be a stable binary an hour later.

On top of the core physics: a GPU vertex-shader grid that physically deforms around massive bodies, a full-sky procedural nebula built from layered fractal noise, and a Roche-limit check that shreds any body that strays too close to something far more massive, spawning debris along its orbital tangent. Software project · Summer 2026

5 D.O.F. Robotic Hand

One servo per finger, driven by gesture or by speech

Every finger assembly was modeled in SolidWorks before it was ever printed, then built as a physical hand with five servos — one per finger, wired to an Arduino on pins 3, 4, 5, 9, and 10. The Arduino owns a tiny serial protocol: a single ASCII character either nudges all five servos by 10 degrees, resets them to a neutral 90-degree pose, or snaps them directly to a named handshape.

Two independent Python pipelines drive that same protocol. The first watches a webcam through MediaPipe's gesture recognizer and nudges the hand whenever it sees a recognized gesture. The second listens to speech, transcribes it, and fingerspells each word letter by letter in an approximation of the ASL manual alphabet — a real, if slow, part of ASL, though this hardware has only one open/curl axis per finger and no thumb opposition, so a handful of letters that differ mainly by finger-crossing (like U vs. V) end up looking near-identical.

Financial AI research · Summer 2025

Multimodal Deep Learning for Financial Market Prediction

Teaching a forecaster to read the news, not just the chart

Most equity forecasters only ever see price history. This study asked what happens if you also give a model language: FinBERT-derived sentiment scores pulled from financial text, fused with the raw market data inside a custom hybrid architecture. Six architectures were benchmarked head-to-head under the same conditions, and ablations were run afterward to isolate exactly which fused features were actually doing the predictive work, rather than just along for the ride.

Adding sentiment wasn't a marginal tweak — it cut forecast error by 34% and doubled R² from 0.34 to 0.68. Toggle the switch below to see that shift the way it showed up in the results: tighter error, and a hybrid model that leaves the five single-modality baselines behind.
